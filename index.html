Before you begin

This is not a diagnosis. It is a way to describe your headaches.

Headache medicine is unusual: there is no blood test and no scan that confirms migraine. A diagnosis is made almost entirely from how you describe your attacks. That puts a lot of weight on a conversation that often lasts ten minutes.

This page walks you through the questions a headache specialist would ask, then hands you an organised summary to bring to that appointment — in the vocabulary clinicians actually use.

Please read this part

  • This cannot diagnose you. It is a structured questionnaire, not a clinician. It has never examined you, cannot see your medical history, and can be confidently wrong.
  • It is not a substitute for medical care — not for a diagnosis, not for treatment, and not for a reason to delay seeing someone.
  • Some headaches are emergencies. If your headache came on like a thunderclap, is the worst of your life, comes with fever and a stiff neck, or with weakness, confusion, or vision loss that has not resolved — stop and seek emergency care now.
  • Never change or stop a prescribed medication because of anything on this page.

What happens to your answers

Nothing leaves your device. There is no account, no database, no analytics, and no cookie. Every answer lives in your browser's memory and is gone the moment you close the tab — which also means there is no way to recover a session, so print or copy your summary before you leave.
